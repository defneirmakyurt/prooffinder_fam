## 2. STATEMENT

This is the exact statement, reproduced verbatim from cell/target.md, l. 3–27.

> Definitions (verbatim from the official statement, run/B/statement.md): a partition of n >= 1 is a weakly
> decreasing sequence lambda = (lambda_1, ..., lambda_s) of positive integers with sum n (s piles).
> The shift B(lambda) is the partition whose parts are the positive numbers among lambda_1 - 1, ..., lambda_s - 1
> together with one extra part equal to s. lambda is cyclic if B^i(lambda) = lambda for some i >= 1.
> T_k = k(k+1)/2, delta_k = (k, k-1, ..., 2, 1). The rank of n >= 1 is the unique k with T_{k-1} < n <= T_k.
>
> Cell text (verbatim): "First, the long-run behaviour: which partitions repeat under the shift, and how they
> fall into cycles. Let n = T_k. Prove that for every partition lambda of n there is an i with
> B^i(lambda) = delta_k, and that delta_k is the only cyclic partition of n. Then let n be arbitrary of rank k,
> say n = T_{k-1} + r with 1 <= r <= k: determine all cyclic partitions of n, and determine the number of
> distinct cycles of B on the partitions of n. Prove both."
>
> Exact targets (all must be proved, for every k >= 1):
>
> (i)  For every k >= 1 and n = T_k: for every partition lambda of n there is an i >= 0 with B^i(lambda) = delta_k;
>      and delta_k is the only cyclic partition of n.
>
> (ii) For every k >= 1 and every r with 1 <= r <= k, n = T_{k-1} + r:
>      (a) give an explicit description (as functions of k and r) of the set of ALL cyclic partitions of n,
>          and prove that a partition of n is cyclic if and only if it lies in that set;
>      (b) give the exact number of distinct cycles of B on the partitions of n (as a formula in k and r),
>          and prove it.
>
> Hand-in: a written proof (LaTeX), stating clearly what is established. Computation may be used only if exhaustive
> over a finite set the argument has reduced the problem to (code included, < 10 min).

**Answer**, as stated in cell/accepted/proof.md (first line, "Statement proved"). Let k ≥ 1, 1 ≤ r ≤ k and n = T_{k-1}+r.
- **(ii)(a).** A partition of n is cyclic if and only if it equals λ(ε) = (k−1+ε_1, k−2+ε_2, …, 1+ε_{k−1}, ε_k), with the last entry deleted when it is 0, for some ε ∈ {0,1}^k with ε_1+…+ε_k = r.
  - Equivalently, it is the staircase δ_{k−1} plus exactly r of the k cells of diagonal D_k.
  - There are exactly C(k,r) such partitions.
  - Source: cell/accepted/proof.md, Theorem 9. The "only if" direction is steps 7.1–7.5; the "if" direction is steps 8.1–8.4; the count comes from step 8.2.
- **Action of B.** B(λ(ε)) = λ(ε_k, ε_1, …, ε_{k−1}), a cyclic rotation of ε (cell/accepted/proof.md, step 8.3).
- **(ii)(b).** The number of distinct cycles is N(k,r) = (1/k) Σ_{d | gcd(k,r)} φ(d) C(k/d, r/d).
  - Source: cell/accepted/proof.md, steps 10.1–10.4.
  - This counts cycles under the reading fixed in step 0.1: a cycle is the forward orbit of a cyclic partition, and cycles are distinct when they are distinct as sets.
- **(i).** For n = T_k (the case r = k), δ_k is the only cyclic partition. Every partition λ of T_k has B^i(λ) = δ_k for some i ≥ 0 (cell/accepted/proof.md, section 11).
- **Consistency check.** At r = k the formula gives N(k,k) = 1 (cell/accepted/proof.md, step 10.4, "Checks").

**Proof structure** (all steps from cell/accepted/proof.md):
1. B maps partitions of n to partitions of n, and every orbit reaches a cyclic partition by pigeonhole (steps 1.1–1.2).
2. The unsorted shift U moves cells by the map R. R preserves the diagonal index j+h−1, so the weight E is unchanged by U (steps 2.1–2.2).
3. Sorting lemma: E(sort c) ≤ E(c), with equality if and only if c is weakly decreasing (Lemma 3, steps 3.1–3.5).
4. E does not increase under B. On a cycle no sorting occurs, so the cell set of B^t λ equals R^t applied to the cell set of λ (steps 4.1–4.2).
5. R rotates each diagonal D_d cyclically, with order d (steps 5.1–5.2).
6. Key lemma: if λ is cyclic and D_d is not contained in its cell set, then no cell of λ lies on D_{d+1}. The proof uses the Chinese remainder theorem with the coprime moduli d and d+1 (Lemma 6).
7. Shape of a cyclic partition: it contains all of D_1 to D_{k−1}, contains exactly r cells of D_k, and has nothing beyond D_k. Hence it equals λ(ε) (steps 7.1–7.5).
8. Converse: every λ(ε) is a partition of n, the map ε ↦ λ(ε) is injective, B acts on λ(ε) by rotating ε, and so every λ(ε) is cyclic (steps 8.1–8.4).
9. Counting cycles: cycles correspond to rotation orbits on binary words. The orbit-counting (Burnside) lemma is proved inline (step 10.2), and the fixed words and gcd classes are counted in steps 10.3–10.4.
10. Part (i) is the case r = k, combined with step 1.2 (section 11).
